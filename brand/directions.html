What all three share

Brand idea

Igwebuike

Igbo for "there is strength in numbers." Many recovered pieces fuse into one strong material. Many suppliers, labs and partners make one dependable chain. The name says brave. The idea says how you get strong.

Positioning

A manufacturer first

Dike Industries makes industrial and defence hardware. Recovered material is how we build it. Testing decides where it goes. This follows your core principle: Dike makes products, and recycled material feeds that work.

Order of claims

Strength first, source second

Lead with what the part does. Then say it's recycled, with the percentage. Buyers pay for performance. Recycled content can win the tie.

The name in English

Teach the sound early

Many English readers will say Dike as "dyke." That's an English word for a flood wall, and it sounds like a slur. You'll hear it on sales calls and in investor meetings, so plan for it now.

Three fixes, lightest to strongest

  • Teach itPrint /dee-keh/ under the name on your site, deck cover and email signature. Say it first in every intro.
  • Use the full nameWrite "Dike Industries" at every first mention. Two words read as a company name.
  • Add an accentDIKÉ tells English readers to sound the last letter. It breaks Igbo spelling, so weigh that first.

How Dike talks

Engineers and procurement teams will read everything you write. Your vision doc already sets the rule: technical honesty. The voice follows it.

Taglines

  • Strength, recovered. My pick

    Short. Holds both halves of the company: what it makes and how.

  • Built brave.

    Plays on the meaning of Dike. Bold, best for campaigns and launch moments.

  • Engineered from Waste. Built for Performance.

    From your vision doc. Leads with waste, which your own rule puts second.

  • Turning Recycled Materials into Industrial Capability.

    From your vision doc. Too long for a tagline. Use it as the descriptor in decks and on the About page.

Keep out of the brand

  • Recycling arrows
  • Leaves and globes
  • Bright eco green
  • Camouflage
  • Weapon silhouettes
  • Stock photos of soldiers
  • Generic "African print" clip art

Each one makes Dike look like a recycler, promises defence work you haven't done yet, or turns Igbo culture into decoration.

Recommendation

Go with B, Uli

  • Your first customers are industrial buyers in Phase 1. B looks like the drawings and spec sheets they already work with.
  • It matches your rule of technical honesty. The look itself says tested.
  • It grows with you. When the defence line arrives, C's heavy type and ochre can become that line's sub-brand.
  • Want more warmth? Add A's bronze as a second accent.
